Statement of Faith

What we believe, and why it shapes everything we build.

The Scriptures

The Bible is the final authority. Not one authority among several.

We hold to the King James Bible as the verbally inspired, inerrant word of God, and the sole authority for everything we teach. Scripture is interpreted literally, in its context, according to its plain meaning — not bent to fit tradition, culture, or personal preference.

Scholars and commentators have a place. Men of serious learning who walked these texts before us are worth hearing. But when a scholar and Scripture conflict, Scripture governs. That is not a caveat. It is the method.

Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path. — Psalm 119:105

Salvation

Freedom from sin, not freedom to sin.

Salvation is by grace, through faith in the finished work of Jesus Christ, not by works. Christ died for sin and rose bodily from the grave, and no man earns his way into that. Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage. — Galatians 5:1

That freedom is not permission. It is freedom from sin's ownership of you, so that you can serve righteousness from the heart instead of from compulsion. For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace. — Romans 6:14

The Christian Life

Convictions are fruit, not rules you impose on yourself.

I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me. — Galatians 2:20. We believe that verse is not a figure of speech. It is the operating reality of the Christian life. A believer does not produce holiness by effort any more than a branch produces its own fruit. He abides in Christ — through Scripture, where God speaks, and prayer, where the believer speaks back — and the Spirit does the rest: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance.

This is why we don't teach Christian liberty as a checklist. Standards imposed from the outside without inward transformation are legalism wearing the language of conviction. And liberty used as cover for the flesh is not liberty — Paul calls it turning grace into an occasion for sin. Both ditches wreck a man. The way between them is a real, sustained walk with God, not a set of rules borrowed from someone else.

For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another. — Galatians 5:13

The Church

The local church has real authority. It is not the final authority.

We believe in submission to church leadership — they watch for your soul and will give account for it, and that is not optional. But a man's conscience stands before God first. When human authority genuinely conflicts with what Scripture plainly says, we ought to obey God rather than men, and we hold that ground with meekness, not defiance.

This is the foundation everything else at Amber & Ember is built on — the app, the seminary, the discipleship program, the books. If you want to go deeper into any of this, start with the Seminary, or reach out directly through Get Involved.
