Statement of Faith

What we believe, and why it shapes everything we build.

The Scriptures

The Bible is the final authority, not one authority among several.

We affirm the King James Bible as the verbally inspired and inerrant word of God. It serves as the sole authority for all teachings presented here. Scripture is interpreted literally and in context, based on its explicit content. Neither tradition, culture, nor subjective feelings are permitted to alter its meaning.

Scholars and commentators contribute important insights, and those who have rigorously studied Scripture before us merit consideration. However, when scholarly interpretations conflict with the Bible, the authority of Scripture prevails. This principle is foundational, not supplementary.

Psalm 119:105 states: thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.

Salvation

Salvation provides freedom from the power of sin, rather than license to continue in sin.

Salvation is granted by grace through faith in the completed work of Jesus Christ, not by human effort. Christ died for sin and rose bodily from the grave; salvation cannot be earned through personal merit. Galatians 5:1 states: stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.

This freedom does not grant permission for self-indulgence. Rather, it is liberation from the dominion of sin, enabling believers to serve righteousness willingly, not out of obligation. Romans 6:14 affirms: for sin shall not have dominion over you, for ye are not under the law, but under grace.

The Christian Life

Personal convictions are the result of spiritual growth, not self-imposed regulations.

Galatians 2:20 states: I am crucified with Christ, nevertheless I live, yet not I, but Christ liveth in me. This is understood as a literal reality, not simply a metaphor. Holiness is not achieved through personal effort, just as a branch does not produce fruit independently. The believer remains connected to Christ through engagement with Scripture and prayer, while the Holy Spirit produces spiritual fruit: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, and self-control.

For this reason, we do not equate Christian liberty with adherence to external checklists. Imposing external standards without inner change constitutes legalism disguised as conviction. Conversely, using liberty to justify self-indulgence is also a misapplication. Paul described this as turning grace into an occasion for sin. Both extremes are spiritually detrimental. Authentic Christian living requires a genuine, ongoing relationship with God, rather than adherence to rules derived from others' convictions.

As my personal relationship with God developed, I critically examined beliefs I had previously accepted without question. Careful study of Scripture clarified my convictions. Galatians 5:13 explains the significance of this process: ye have been called unto liberty, only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another.

The Church

The local church possesses genuine authority, but it is not the ultimate authority.

We affirm the importance of submitting to church leadership, as leaders are responsible for the congregation's spiritual well-being and will be held accountable for their stewardship. However, individual conscience is ultimately accountable to God. When leadership directives conflict with Scripture, Acts 5:29 provides guidance: we ought to obey God rather than men. Maintaining this position requires humility rather than defiance.

All initiatives established here, including the app, discipleship program, and publications, are founded upon these principles. For further engagement, individuals are encouraged to begin with Discipleship or connect directly through Get Involved.
