Understanding the Holy Spirit

He isn’t a feeling or a force. He’s God, and He lives in every believer.

Who He Is

A Person, not a force you tap into.

A lot of people think of the Holy Spirit as some kind of energy, something Christians tap into when they need a boost. That isn’t what the Bible teaches. The Holy Spirit is a Person. Jesus called Him the Comforter and talked about Him as he, not it: But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you (John 14:26). Paul says grieve not the holy Spirit of God (Ephesians 4:30). You can’t grieve a force. You can only grieve a person.

And He isn’t just a person. He’s God. When Ananias lied about the money he’d kept back, Peter told him he had lied to the Holy Ghost, and in the next breath said, thou hast not lied unto men, but unto God (Acts 5:3-4). Peter didn’t treat those as two different things.

The moment you’re saved, He comes to live in you. Paul says ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, which is the earnest of our inheritance (Ephesians 1:13-14). An earnest is a down payment. It’s God’s guarantee that everything He promised is coming. What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you (1 Corinthians 6:19). He isn’t somewhere far off. He lives where you live.

What He Does

He teaches, convicts, and produces fruit you can't fake.

He teaches you. Jesus said, when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth (John 16:13). Paul says the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him (1 Corinthians 2:14). That’s why the Bible can read like a closed book before someone is saved and then start opening up after. The words didn’t change. The Teacher showed up.

He convicts you. Jesus said that when the Spirit comes, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment (John 16:8). Conviction feels different from shame. Shame tells you to hide from God. Conviction tells you the truth and points you back to Him, more like a friend being honest with you than someone accusing you. If you’re saved and you do something wrong and it bothers you, that’s Him.

And He produces fruit. But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance: against such there is no law (Galatians 5:22-23). That list isn’t a checklist to work through. It’s what shows up when someone stays close to God, the way fruit shows up on a healthy tree without the tree straining for it. Jesus said, I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing (John 15:5). A branch doesn’t try harder to grow fruit. It stays connected to the vine.

Staying Close

You can’t lose Him, but you can ignore Him.

Once the Holy Spirit lives in you, He doesn’t leave. But that doesn’t mean you’ll always hear Him. Paul warns, Quench not the Spirit (1 Thessalonians 5:19). You can put out a fire by smothering it, and you can smother the Spirit’s work in your life by ignoring Him, staying out of the Bible, and getting comfortable with sin He’s already convicted you of.

Staying close to Him works like any relationship. He speaks to you through Scripture, and you speak to Him in prayer. If you stop doing either one, you’ll drift. If you keep at both, day after day, you’ll find He’s been there the whole time. If you don’t know where to start, start with how to read the Bible and how to pray.
