Understanding Salvation

What God actually does when He saves someone, and why none of it depends on you.

Not Earned

Grace, through faith. Not by anything you did.

For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast. That’s Ephesians 2:8-9, and it shuts down two wrong ideas at once. You didn’t earn salvation by being good enough. And you can’t lose it by not being good enough afterward. If you could lose it by failing, then you’d be the one keeping yourself saved, and that’s right back to works.

Think about how a gift works. If I hand you a gift and then tell you to pay me for it, it isn’t a gift anymore. It’s a sale. Paul makes the same point in Romans 11:6: And if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace. You can’t mix the two. Either God saved you or you saved yourself, and the Bible is clear which one it is.

Christ died for sin and rose bodily from the grave. That really happened, and it’s the whole basis for salvation. On the cross Jesus said, It is finished (John 19:30). He didn’t say it was mostly finished and you’d have to do the rest. Nothing you do adds to it, and nothing was ever missing that you needed to complete.

What Happens

More happens in that moment than most people realize.

First, your sin is forgiven and God counts you righteous. Not just neutral, righteous. For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him (2 Corinthians 5:21). Christ took your sin, and you get His righteousness. Nobody would make that trade except God. Romans 5:1 says it this way: Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. The war between you and God is over.

Second, the Holy Spirit moves in. Paul tells the Ephesians that after ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise (Ephesians 1:13). A seal is a mark of ownership. God put His mark on you, and He doesn’t take it back. We go further into this on Understanding the Holy Spirit.

And you become His child. But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name (John 1:12). You aren’t a servant trying to stay on good terms with the boss. You’re family. A kid can disappoint his father, and a good father will correct him, but that kid doesn’t stop being his son.

Freedom, Not Permission

Saved from sin's ownership, not turned loose to do whatever you want.

For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace. Romans 6:14 doesn’t say sin stops existing. It says sin stops owning you. Before you were saved, the best you could do was obey God because you had to. Now you have a heart that actually wants to.

Some people hear that salvation can’t be lost and decide that means they can live however they want. Paul saw that coming. What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein? (Romans 6:1-2). Grace isn’t a license. If someone says they’re saved but has no desire at all to change, I’d ask them to take an honest look at whether anything ever really happened. Paul told the Corinthians the same thing: Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith (2 Corinthians 13:5).

That’s why salvation changes how a person lives, not just what they believe on paper. You won’t be perfect. You’ll still fall. But you won’t be able to fall comfortably anymore, because the Spirit living in you won’t let you.
